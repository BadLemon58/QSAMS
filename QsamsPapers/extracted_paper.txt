Chapter 1
Introduction
Background of the Project 
In the field of Information Technology, digital technologies simplify routine processes by automating repetitive tasks and storing information in a centralized system. Attendance of students is an essential element of class management since it enables teachers to monitor the participation of their students as well as collect accurate attendance records. One of the technologies that can be used for this purpose is the Quick Response (QR) code, which facilitates the efficient recording of attendance information through QR code scanning. This process can save class time and make attendance checking more convenient and manageable [1,2].
The application of QR code technology for attendance monitoring has been adopted by various educational institutions around the world. Internationally, the University of Mataram in Indonesia developed an Android-based attendance system that uses QR code technology to record student attendance. The system allows students to scan QR codes while enabling lecturers to monitor attendance more efficiently and reduce proxy attendance through QR code and location verification [3]. In Philippines, General Emilio Aguinaldo National High School in Imus City, Cavite has developed the application of QR codes in classroom attendance [4], while Jesus F. Magsaysay Technical Vocational High School has researched on QR code identification cards for attendance monitoring [5]. It is evident from the above that QR technology is an effective substitute to traditional methods of attendance. At present, there is limited evidence of the implementation of QR code-based attendance monitoring systems among educational institutions in Midsayap. 
Currently, attendance can be taken by doing things like calling out names, having an attendance sheet, and manual data collection. Although these are simple ways to do so, they might waste time during lessons, especially with too many students. Also, manual collection of data can sometimes lead to errors or incomplete data. There might also be problems with attendance forgery, as students who are not in class might have another one report their attendance for them. To address these issues, a better method needs to be developed [6,2].
To address these challenges, the researchers propose the development of the QR Code-Based Student Attendance Monitoring System (QSAMS), which combines QR code generation, scanning, attendance recording, and monitoring in one platform. The system automatically refreshes QR codes at set intervals to help prevent unauthorized attendance recording [7,8]. It is designed to make attendance checking faster, more secure, and more convenient for both teachers and students [9]. Teachers can easily create class lists using a QR code or class code, while attendance can be recorded by scanning a student's personal QR code or a QR code generated for the class. To make sure only students who are actually in the classroom can check in, the system only accepts scans from devices within the allowed range. The proposed system will be developed for Notre Dame of Midsayap College (NDMC), with teachers and students as its primary users.
QSAMS will be developed as a Progressive Web Application (PWA) using React for the front-end, Vite as the build tool, Tailwind CSS for the user interface, and Supabase with PostgreSQL for secure data storage and management. This technology stack will support efficient QR code generation, attendance recording, user authentication, and attendance monitoring for both teachers and students. 
1.2 Objectives of the Project
The primary objective of this project is to develop a QR code-based attendance management system that makes attendance checking more organized, more secure, faster, and easier for teachers while allowing students to conveniently monitor their attendance records.

Monitor and review students’ attendance record.
Generate attendance record.
Students: 
Update the profile picture and change the password.
Enter classroom through QR code and code.

View attendance record.

The project will be advantageous for the following recipients:

They will be able to manage this without having to manually record the details, but by using a computerized method that organizes their attendance data. This will give them more time to teach in class. 
Students. Students will have convenient access to their attendance records, allowing them to monitor their attendance status, view their attendance history, and keep track of their class participation. This will encourage students to become more responsible and accountable for maintaining regular attendance.

By replacing manual attendance with a digital system, the college can improve record management, reduce paperwork, and support its goal of integrating modern technology into academic operations.
1.4 Scope and Delimitations of the Project
The study focuses on the development of the QR Code-Based Student Attendance Monitoring System (QSAMS) for Notre Dame of Midsayap College (NDMC). The system will be developed as a Progressive Web Application (PWA) that can be accessed through a web browser or installed on compatible mobile devices for added convenience. It is designed for authorized teachers and enrolled students of the institution, providing a more efficient and secure alternative to the traditional attendance process. By integrating QR code technology, user authentication, and a secure cloud database into a single platform, QSAMS aims to simplify attendance management while ensuring the accuracy and reliability of attendance records.
The system will provide teachers with tools to efficiently manage attendance and class records. Teachers will be able to create class lists using both QR code and unique class code, allowing students to join their assigned classes quickly and conveniently. During each class session, teachers may either scan a student's personal QR code or generate a QR code for students to scan when recording their attendance. To enhance attendance security, the generated QR code will automatically refresh after a specified period, and the system will only accept attendance scans from devices within the designated range to help ensure that only students who are physically present can check in. In addition, teachers will be able to review attendance records, monitor student attendance, and generate attendance reports whenever needed.
Students will be able to join classes using the class code provided by their teacher and record their attendance through QR code scanning. The system will also allow them to view their attendance history, attendance rate, total sessions attended, and recorded absences, enabling them to keep track of their attendance throughout the semester. All attendance records will be securely stored in the system's cloud database, providing both teachers and students with convenient access to accurate and up-to-date attendance information.
However, this study has several limitations. The proposed system is intended only for authorized teachers and enrolled students of Notre Dame of Midsayap College (NDMC). It is designed specifically for attendance monitoring and does not cover other academic functions such as enrollment, grading, class scheduling, or student information management. Since QSAMS is a web-based system, a stable internet connection is required to access its features and keep attendance records up to date. In addition, QR code scanning requires a compatible device with a working camera. The system is also limited to its own attendance management functions and does not support integration with other institutional systems or third-party educational platforms.
1.5 Definition of Terms
The following terms are defined conceptually and operationally to clarify the key terms used in the QR Code-Based Student Attendance Monitoring System (QSAMS).
Attendance History. Refers to the record of a student's attendance, including the dates and status of attendance for each class session. In QSAMS, it allows students and teachers to review previous attendance records.
Attendance Monitoring. Refers to the process of recording, tracking, and managing student attendance. In QSAMS, attendance monitoring is performed through QR code scanning and stored digitally for easy access and reporting.
Attendance Rate. Refers to the percentage of classes attended by a student based on the total number of class sessions. In QSAMS, this information is automatically calculated and displayed for students and teachers.
Authorized Teacher. Refers to a registered faculty member who has permission to access QSAMS. Teachers can create classes, manage student attendance, generate QR codes, view attendance records, and generate attendance reports.
Class Code. Refers to a unique code generated for each class that allows students to join their assigned class. In QSAMS, students can join a class by entering the class code or by scanning the corresponding class join QR code.
Class Join QR Code. Refers to the QR code generated for a specific class that allows enrolled students to quickly join the class by scanning it. In QSAMS, students may join a class either by scanning the class join QR code or by entering the corresponding class code.
Cloud Database. Refers to the online database used to securely store and manage user information, class data, and attendance records. QSAMS uses a cloud database to ensure that attendance information is updated and accessible whenever an internet connection is available.
Enrolled Student. Refers to a student officially registered in a class who is authorized to access QSAMS. Students can join classes, record their attendance, and view their attendance records through the system.
Notre Dame of Midsayap College (NDMC). Refers to the educational institution in Midsayap, Cotabato, where the proposed QR Code-Based Student Attendance Monitoring System (QSAMS) will be developed and evaluated.
PostgreSQL. Refers to the relational database management system used by QSAMS to store and organize user information, class data, and attendance records securely.
Progressive Web Application (PWA). Refers to a web application that can be accessed through a web browser or installed on compatible mobile devices. In QSAMS, the PWA allows teachers and students to use the system conveniently without requiring a traditional mobile application.
QR Code (Quick Response Code). Refers to a two-dimensional barcode capable of storing digital information that can be scanned using a compatible device. In QSAMS, QR codes are used to record student attendance quickly and accurately.
QR Code-Based Student Attendance Monitoring System (QSAMS). Refers to the proposed attendance management system developed for Notre Dame of Midsayap College. The system uses QR code technology to record, monitor, and manage student attendance while providing teachers and students with a faster, more organized, and secure attendance process.
React. Refers to the JavaScript library used to develop the user interface of QSAMS. It provides a responsive and interactive environment for teachers and students when using the system.
Supabase. Refers to the cloud-based backend platform used by QSAMS for user authentication, database management, and secure storage of attendance records.
Tailwind CSS. Refers to the utility-first CSS framework used to design the user interface of QSAMS, providing a responsive, consistent, and user-friendly appearance across different devices.
User Authentication. Refers to the process of verifying the identity of teachers and students before allowing access to the system. In QSAMS, user authentication helps ensure that only authorized users can access attendance records and system features.
Users. Refers to all registered individuals who interact with QSAMS, specifically authorized teachers and enrolled students. Each user is provided with appropriate access based on their assigned role.
Vite. Refers to the build tool used in developing QSAMS. It provides a fast development environment and improves the performance of the web application during development and deployment.

2.1 Related Literature
Current Practices in Student Attendance Monitoring
Student attendance is traditionally recorded by calling students’ names, using paper attendance sheets, and manually marking attendance. While these methods are commonly used, they require teachers to check and record students individually, which can consume class time, especially when there are many students. Manual recording also makes attendance records more dependent on careful and consistent handling by the teacher [11,12].
Traditional attendance practices may also result in several concerns, including recording mistakes, incomplete attendance records, and difficulty in organizing records from different class sessions. There is also a risk of proxy attendance, where a student may have another student mark attendance on their behalf. These concerns can make it difficult for teachers to ensure that attendance records accurately reflect who was actually present in class. [13,14].
International studies have identified concerns associated with these traditional practices. At King Saud University, the study on student attendance addressed the limitations of conventional attendance-taking, particularly the need to manually record students' attendance during class sessions [11]. At UiTM Cawangan Terengganu, the development of an attendance system was also related to concerns with the existing manual process of recording student attendance [12]. Another study on attendance tracking discussed the difficulty of relying on conventional attendance procedures and the need to address issues associated with attendance recording [13]. In addition, the University of Technology Malaysia examined the concern of unauthorized attendance recording, which may occur when the attendance process does not have sufficient verification or security measures [14].
Local studies also reflect concerns with traditional attendance monitoring. At Jesus F. Magsaysay Technical Vocational High School, the study focused on student attendance monitoring using QR-code identification cards, indicating concerns with the existing method of monitoring attendance [5]. Another study from the University of the Philippines addressed the concern of verifying student presence by incorporating an SMS location tracker with QR-code attendance [15]. These studies indicate that traditional attendance procedures may raise concerns not only with recording attendance but also with confirming whether the student being recorded is actually present.
The limitations of traditional attendance monitoring show the need for a more organized and reliable method of recording student attendance. Issues such as time-consuming procedures, recording errors, incomplete records, and proxy attendance can affect the accuracy of attendance information. These concerns serve as the basis for developing QSAMS: Mobile and Web-based Classroom Attendance Monitoring using QR Codes for Notre Dame of Midsayap College, which aims to provide teachers and students with a more organized, accurate, and secure way of managing attendance.
Advantages of QR Code-Based Attendance Monitoring
QR code-based attendance systems provide several advantages over traditional attendance methods. One of the main benefits is that attendance can be recorded through a simple scan using a mobile device. This can make the process faster and more convenient for both teachers and students. It also reduces the need for teachers to call students one by one, giving them more time to focus on their lessons. In addition, attendance information can be recorded digitally, making it easier to organize and manage [11,12].
Another important advantage is improved accuracy. Since attendance is recorded digitally, there is less dependence on manually writing or marking each student's attendance. This can help reduce common recording mistakes and make attendance information more consistent. Digital attendance systems can also make records easier to store, retrieve, and review when needed [6].
QR code attendance systems can also include security and verification features. Some systems use additional information to help confirm that a student is actually present during an attendance session. For example, Universitas Klabat combined QR codes with geolocation to provide another way of checking a student's location during attendance [16]. The University of Technology Malaysia (UTM) also used a secret code as an additional security measure to help prevent unauthorized attendance recording [14]. These features show how QR technology can be combined with other methods to make attendance monitoring more reliable.
Several international institutions have demonstrated the advantages of QR code-based attendance systems. King Saud University used QR codes to allow students to record attendance by scanning a code containing course and lecture information, making the process faster and more convenient [11]. At Universiti Teknologi MARA (UiTM) Cawangan Terengganu, QR codes were used to record attendance digitally, making attendance checking simpler and more organized [12]. Kolej Komuniti Segamat also used an online QR-code attendance system that allowed attendance information to be stored electronically, making records easier to manage and retrieve [17]. These examples show how QR technology can improve the way attendance is recorded and managed.
Local institutions have also shown the benefits of using QR codes for attendance monitoring. Jesus F. Magsaysay Technical Vocational High School explored QR-code identification cards as an electronic method of recording student attendance, making attendance monitoring more manageable [5]. The University of the Philippines also combined QR-code attendance with an SMS location tracker, providing an additional way to verify student presence during attendance [15]. These examples show that QR technology can be useful in local educational settings by making attendance recording more organized while supporting verification.
The advantages shown by these existing systems provide a basis for the development of QSAMS. The proposed system applies QR-code scanning to make attendance recording faster, easier, and more organized while maintaining digital attendance records for accurate monitoring. It also includes automatically refreshed QR codes and designated device-range verification to help reduce unauthorized attendance recording. By bringing these features together in one platform, QSAMS aims to provide teachers and students at Notre Dame of Midsayap College with a more convenient, accurate, organized, and secure way of managing classroom attendance [11,14,15,16].

QSAMS: Features and Functions
QR codes are widely used in different areas because they provide a quick way to access or share digital information. They can be used to open websites, access digital documents, share contact information, view digital menus, and provide other types of information through a simple scan. Instead of manually entering a link, code, or other details, users can scan the QR code using a compatible device to access the information more quickly. The use of QR codes in different applications shows their flexibility as a convenient way of connecting physical information with digital content [1], [2].
QR codes can also be applied to student attendance monitoring because they allow attendance information to be captured through scanning. In an attendance system, a QR code can contain information that identifies a student, class, or attendance session. Once scanned, the system can record the attendance digitally, reducing the need for teachers to manually write or mark attendance. This approach has been applied in different attendance systems, showing that QR-code scanning can be used as a method of recording and managing student attendance [11], [12].
King Saud University developed a QR-code-based attendance system that allowed students to scan QR codes using their mobile devices to record attendance. The QR code contained course and lecture information to identify the particular attendance session, providing a digital alternative to traditional attendance procedures [11].
Universiti Teknologi MARA (UiTM) Cawangan Terengganu developed a QR-code-based attendance system to record student attendance digitally. The system applied QR-code scanning as an alternative to manual attendance procedures, making attendance information easier to record and manage electronically [12].
Universitas Klabat implemented an attendance system that combined QR codes with geolocation technology. Students could scan a QR code to record their attendance, while location information was used to help verify their physical presence during the attendance session [16].
Kolej Komuniti Segamat implemented an online student attendance system using QR codes to record and store attendance information electronically. The system applied QR-code scanning to provide a more organized way of managing student attendance records [17].
The Scandence system applied QR-code technology to attendance management. It was developed as a QR-code-based attendance management system, demonstrating another approach to digitally recording student attendance [18].
In the Philippines, the University of the Philippines developed a QR-code attendance system that incorporated an SMS location tracker. The location information provided an additional method for verifying a student's presence during attendance [15].
At Jesus F. Magsaysay Technical Vocational High School, QR-code identification cards were explored as a method of monitoring student attendance. The system used QR codes as an electronic means of recording attendance and provided an alternative to traditional attendance monitoring [5].
Abas National High School in Sallapadan, Abra also explored the use of a QR-code-based attendance system. The system was intended to provide a digital approach to recording and monitoring student attendance [1].
The reviewed systems provide different features that can be considered in the development of QSAMS. King Saud University and UiTM demonstrate the use of QR codes for recording attendance, while Universitas Klabat and the University of the Philippines incorporate location-based verification. Kolej Komuniti Segamat and Scandence demonstrate the use of digital attendance records. QSAMS builds on these concepts by combining QR-code generation and scanning, class management, digital attendance recording, attendance monitoring, and security features in one Progressive Web Application. It also includes automatically refreshed QR codes and designated device-range verification to help reduce unauthorized attendance recording.
2.3 System Development Life Cycle (SDLC)
The System Development Life Cycle (SDLC) provides a structured approach for planning, designing, developing, and implementing a software system. For the development of the QR Code-Based Student Attendance Monitoring System (QSAMS), the researchers selected the Waterfall Model as the development approach. The Waterfall Model follows a sequential process in which each phase is completed before moving to the next. This approach is suitable for QSAMS because the system has clearly identified requirements and functions, including user authentication, class management, QR code generation and scanning, attendance recording, attendance monitoring, and report generation.
2.3.1 Planning and Requirements Gathering
The first phase focuses on identifying the needs of the intended users and determining the requirements of the system. For QSAMS, the researchers identified the common difficulties encountered in traditional attendance procedures, such as time-consuming manual recording, possible recording errors, and attendance forgery. The researchers also determined the needs of teachers and students as the primary users of the system. Based on these requirements, the system will include features for managing classes and students, generating and scanning QR codes, recording attendance, monitoring attendance records, and allowing students to view their attendance history. The technical requirements of the system, including the use of a Progressive Web Application (PWA), React, Vite, Tailwind CSS, Supabase, and PostgreSQL, were also considered during this phase.
2.3.2 System and Architecture Design
The second phase focuses on designing the overall structure, architecture, database, user interface, and flow of information within QSAMS. The system is designed as a Progressive Web Application (PWA) that allows teachers and students to access the system through a web browser using smartphones or laptops. The architecture separates the presentation and application functions from data storage and management to provide an organized flow of information between users, the system, and the database.
The frontend of QSAMS is developed using React, with Vite serving as the development and build tool. Tailwind CSS is used to create a responsive and consistent user interface, while React Router DOM manages navigation between the different pages and functions of the system. Lucide React provides the interface icons used throughout the application. For QR-code functions, qrcode.react is used to generate QR codes, while html5-qrcode supports QR-code scanning through the device camera. These technologies work together to provide an interactive and responsive interface for both teachers and students.
The backend of QSAMS uses Supabase as the cloud-based backend platform. It handles user authentication, communication with the database, and management of system data. Supabase provides the services needed to process user accounts, class information, enrollment data, attendance sessions, and attendance records. PostgreSQL serves as the relational database that stores and organizes the system's data. This backend structure allows the frontend to communicate with the stored data and perform functions such as creating classes, joining classes, recording attendance, and retrieving attendance records.
The architecture also defines separate access and functions for teachers and students. Teachers can manage classes and students, generate and scan QR codes, manage attendance, monitor attendance records, and generate reports. Students can join classes using a class code or QR code, scan or generate QR codes as required, and view their attendance records. Attendance information passes from the user's device through the QSAMS application and backend services before being stored in the PostgreSQL database.
The system architecture also incorporates QR-code-based attendance verification and security features. QR codes used for attendance are automatically refreshed at set intervals, while attendance scans are restricted to devices within the designated range. These features are designed to help reduce unauthorized attendance recording and improve the reliability of attendance verification. The overall architecture provides an organized connection between the user interface, application functions, backend services, QR-code components, and database, supporting the main attendance monitoring functions of QSAMS.
2.3.3 Development and Implementation
The third phase involves developing the actual QSAMS application based on the approved system design. The researchers will implement the system as a Progressive Web Application using React for the user interface, Vite as the development and build tool, and Tailwind CSS for the system's design. Supabase and PostgreSQL will be used for authentication, data management, and storage of attendance records. The development will include the implementation of teacher and student accounts, class management, QR code generation and scanning, attendance recording, attendance monitoring, and attendance reporting. Security features such as automatically refreshed QR codes and designated device-range verification will also be implemented to help reduce unauthorized attendance recording.
The Waterfall Model was selected because it provides a clear and organized sequence for developing QSAMS. The requirements and major functions of the system have already been identified, making it appropriate to proceed through the development phases in a structured manner. Each phase provides a foundation for the succeeding phase, which helps the researchers maintain consistency between the system requirements, design, and implementation. This approach also makes it easier to document the development process and monitor the progress of the project. For QSAMS, the Waterfall Model provides a practical development structure for producing a functional attendance monitoring system that addresses the identified needs of teachers and students at Notre Dame of Midsayap College.

Table 2.2 provides an overview of the software requirements for developing QSAMS, encompassing various technologies to ensure a reliable, efficient, and user-friendly student attendance monitoring system. The system will run on Windows 10 or Windows 11 64-bit, providing a stable environment for development and operation. Antigravity IDE will serve as the main development environment for writing, testing, and managing the system’s source code. For the front-end development, React v19 will be used to create a responsive and interactive interface, while Vite v8 will support the development and build process of the application. Tailwind CSS v4 will be utilized to design a consistent and responsive user interface, while React Router DOM will support navigation between the different pages of QSAMS. Morphicons will also be used to provide icons that improve the appearance and usability of the system.
For the QR code functions, qrcode.react will be used to generate QR codes, while html5-qrcode will support QR code scanning for attendance recording. Supabase will serve as the backend platform for handling the system’s backend services, while PostgreSQL will be used to store and manage user information, class records, and attendance data. Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Mozilla Firefox, and Safari will allow teachers and students to access QSAMS through their devices as a Progressive Web Application (PWA). Git and GitHub will be used for version control, allowing the development team to manage and track changes throughout the development process. For documentation and data organization, Microsoft Word will be used to prepare project documents and reports, while Microsoft Excel will support data organization and analysis. Together, these software technologies provide a well-structured foundation for the development, testing, deployment, and maintenance of QSAMS, supporting its main attendance monitoring functions and operational requirements.
2.6 Ethical Considerations
The development of QSAMS considers ethical principles to ensure a secure, fair, and transparent approach to managing student attendance and personal information. By giving importance to privacy, data security, and fairness, the system aims to provide a responsible way of recording and monitoring attendance at Notre Dame of Midsayap College (NDMC). Key ethical measures include proper user authentication and role-based access control to prevent unauthorized access and protect the accuracy of attendance records. User information and attendance data should be handled carefully and accessed only by authorized users.
The system also promotes transparency and accountability by maintaining accurate attendance records and ensuring that attendance information is properly recorded through the QR code scanning process. The user-friendly interface allows teachers and students with different levels of technical experience to use the system more easily. In addition, the collection and management of student information should be limited to what is necessary for the attendance functions of QSAMS. By following these ethical practices, QSAMS supports responsible data management and helps maintain the trust of teachers and students in the attendance process.
